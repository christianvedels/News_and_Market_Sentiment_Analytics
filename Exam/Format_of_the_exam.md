# Exam in News and Market Sentiment Analytics, 2026

- The exam runs from 13 December 2026, 12:00 to 20 December 2026, 12:00. Show that you can use NLP tools as taught in the course to gain relevant and useful insights. The format is open. You will answer a question of the form:
  + "Demonstrate the skills you have learned in this course to gain relevant and useful insights into [x]"

- [x], or a source of [x], will be provided. Examples of what [x] could be:
  + The corpus of Reuters articles from a collection
  + Financial news that you find yourself
  + Trap Danmark, which you scrape from http://runeberg.org/trap/2-1/0053.html

- You submit the exam as a pdf containing:
  + A paper describing the **objective** of your project, the **NLP tools** you used and why they are **appropriate**, a **demonstration of the results** and **how well your method performs** (e.g. against a sample you label by hand).
  + Documentation: **a link to a GitHub repository** or **a well-documented appendix with code**.
  + References to the literature you rely on. Follow good academic practice and cite all sources.

- Everything can be done with free tools (see the [README](https://github.com/christianvedels/News_and_Market_Sentiment_Analytics)).

## AI tools
You may use generative AI and coding agents (ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot and similar). If you do, declare how you used them, as SDU requires ([SDU's guidance](https://sdunet.dk/en/undervisning-og-eksamen/uddannelse/vejledning)).

I grade your project with these tools in mind. With them, a week is enough for analyses that used to take months, so I expect more than in earlier years. Show that you can do modern news and market sentiment analysis: choose a sensible method, validate it and interpret the results.

A report generated with little thought is usually generic and poorly validated, and it will get a poor grade. Effort shows in your choices, in how you check your results and in the insights you draw.

You get the best results when the ideas and first drafts come from you, and AI improves them, not the other way around. This holds for the text, the choice of methods and the code. Decide what you want to find out and how, draft it, and then let AI check, correct and extend your work. You can also ask AI for inspiration, but beware: AI has terrible taste. If AI sets the direction and you only edit, the project tends to stay generic.
